# A recurrence report does not override the ladder

## Context

`a-repair-revalidates-the-evidence-it-moved` reached `review` with a current revision, a passing ledger, and no strict-bar finding. Its historical `instrument-unsound` recurrence was correctly detected as `stopLineCandidate`, but `src/workflow/navigation.ts` routed that diagnostic to `/kata-build`. `src/workflow/repair-entry.ts` then correctly refused Build: it admits a review repair only for an actual blocking/major finding, a ledger deficit, or a superseded revision. The result was an unexecutable loop.

## Decision

Keep recurrence detection and reporting, but do not let a historical `stopLineCandidate` override the normal review ladder. A recurrence with no current strict-bar finding must lead to the fresh review needed to decide whether the repair actually resolved it. A clean current review then follows the normal Judge path. Build admission remains unchanged; no synthetic finding or waiver is created.

**One rule, two branches.** `src/workflow/navigation.ts` holds two recurrence routes, not one: `review_class_recurring` (a class recurring inside a repair) and `review_loop_recurring` (a finding *id* recurring across revisions). Both were written as `phase === 'review' && <diagnostic>`, and both had the same shape — a reading about history overriding the ladder, whose destination refuses in exactly the state that triggers it. The repair is therefore stated once, as a predicate the two branches share (`ladderAdmitsRepair`), rather than at the site the measurement happened to land on. Fixing one instance and leaving its sibling is the way this class returns in the next round.

The predicate reads one of the facts `repair-entry.ts` also reads when it admits a repair — a blocking finding at the tier's bar, or a major one where the tier blocks on major findings — and it is a **proper subset** of that admission, which additionally admits a ledger deficit and a superseded seal. So a route this change keeps open is not the same thing as a state the entry admits; the two agree on the case this change is about and are allowed to differ elsewhere. Where it holds, the recurrence branches still route and still name their own reason (a class recurring inside a repair says more than one id repeating); where it does not, the recurrence stays a published reading and the ladder's own branch decides.

**Both conjuncts of the branch conditions survive, including the phase.** The branches used to read `phase === 'review' && <diagnostic>`, and the first version of this change replaced that conjunct with the new predicate — so a recurrence in `judge` or `hardVerify` would have been routed by a rule about reviews, where the authority is `authorizeJudgeRepair` / `authorizeVerifyRepair` and their preconditions differ. Measured by an independent verifier as a *file:line* observation, then reproduced here as a failing case: `phase: 'judge'` with a blocking finding and a live recurrence answered `/kata-build` `review_class_recurring`. The guard is back on both branches, and each branch has its own case for it — the first version asserted the guard through the class route alone, so removing the id route's conjunct stayed green.

**Two of this change's own guards were decorative until an independent verifier said so.** It mutated the predicate down to its blocking half and every selector it tried stayed green: nothing asked the admission question in a state that was *both* recurring and major-only, so the `strict`-blocks-on-major half could have been deleted without a reading. Both halves are now exercised, and the two mutations (drop the major half; drop each phase guard) are declared falsifiers.

## Acceptance and tests

- **AC-1:** Recurrence remains visible in diagnostics but cannot route a current, otherwise-repair-free review to Build.
- **AC-2:** That state requests one fresh independent review without creating a repair obligation or bypassing the stop-line.
- **AC-3:** A clean fresh review reaches Judge rather than looping back to review or Build because of historical recurrence.
- **AC-4:** Build remains unavailable unless its existing strict admission conditions are genuinely met.

`tests/unit/a-recurrence-report-does-not-override-the-ladder.test.ts` builds each state through `readUpstreamSummary` on disk — a sealed revision, an evidence item and a round history carrying the recurring class — rather than assembling a summary by hand, so the states are the ones the production reader produces. Its cases cover the class route (AC-1, AC-2), a clean recorded review (AC-3), and the two directions of AC-4: the same diagnostic still routes where a blocking finding bound to the current revision makes the repair admissible, and `authorizeRepair` refuses in the state with nothing at the bar. A fifth case pins the id reading against the same rule, so neither branch can be re-opened alone.

Single-point mutations, each proven parseable and byte-restored: the class branch ignoring the predicate reddens 3 cases; the id branch ignoring it reddens 1; the predicate forced to admit reddens 4; forced to refuse reddens 1 in the new selector and 3 in the pre-existing one.

**Two review rounds measured two more shapes in the branch guards, and both were repaired here rather than recorded.** The first: only the `oracle === 'mechanizable'` half of the stop-line filter was driven through the route, so a route re-derived as `(entry) => entry.oracle === 'mechanizable'` — one half kept, the other dropped — stayed green across the whole suite; the origin half now has its own case, which asserts the reading (`oracle` satisfied, `repairOrigin === 0`, the ladder admitting) so it cannot pass for another reason. The second: a sentence about the published priority described an ordering the numbers do not decide — the class route publishes `2150 + candidates` against `2100 + ids`, so the crossover is `ids > 50 + candidates`, and the formula is now measured at one and at two candidates rather than stated.

**Why five review rounds kept finding the same class, and what the fix was.** From the second round on, every round returned one class: a *claim whose subject was the router's answer in a state* rather than the branch this change touches. A state-level claim inherits the whole ladder's precedence, and this router has some thirty branches, so each round could name one more branch above the state I had described — the ledger branch (round 3), the invariants table's `repair_failed_verify` entry (round 4), and a fifth was still running when this was written. The evidence never had that problem: every falsifier is a single-point mutation of a branch's own guard, and none has ever been falsified. That is the diagnosis — **the prose had drifted off the branch while the evidence stayed on it** — and the remedy is therefore the claim form rather than more cases. C1–C9 each now name a *branch, a derivation, or a branch-local publication*; none states what the router answers in a state; and the router's order is deliberately not restated here, because it lives in `suggestCandidateAction`'s own switch and array. The state fixtures in the selector remain as demonstrations of the acceptance criteria, not as claims about that order.

**The route is only fixed when its destination opens.** The AC-4 control asserted the router's route and the entry's *refusal* in the empty state, so the positive pairing — the state the class route fires in is one `authorizeRepair('review', …)` admits — was claimed by its comment and asserted nowhere. Both directions are now asserted, because a route pointing at a door that refuses is precisely the defect this change removes.

**A third round corrected the noun.** The class-recurrence route is a plain repair route: `review_class_recurring` is `null` in `trustBoundaryByReason` and needs no confirmation, while `escalate_review_without_progress` is the stop-line escalation and a different branch. Claim C9 had called the route an escalation — first as a noun, then as a verb; `stopLineCandidate` is a report and neither `classRecurrence` nor its caller refuses a round.

## Consequences for an existing contract

`tests/unit/a-recurring-review-loop-is-reported.test.ts` (the contract the archived change `the-review-loop-carries-its-defect-classes` left) asserted the two recurrence routes in a state carrying **no review record at all**, so the ladder had nothing to repair and the route it asserted was one `kata-cli build` refuses. Its fixture now carries a blocking finding bound to the sealed revision — the state that makes the route walkable — so the property it names is still pinned rather than weakened. Two things in that fixture had to become real for the reader to accept it: the task record is created through `createTask` instead of written by hand (the router never opened it through the schema until a case carried a review record), and the new option is `blockingFinding`.


## Review history, and what is *not* certified here

The change took five review rounds. Rounds 1–4 each returned findings and each was repaired: round 1 three notes, round 2 one major (a boundary stated against the id count alone) plus a minor and three notes, round 3 one major (a claim asserting a route in the state the ladder admits nothing) plus three minors, round 4 two majors (a claim omitting the invariants table, and the same boundary unqualified by the candidate count) plus a minor and a note. Every repair came with a case and a single-point mutation; the declared falsifiers were re-read at each new subject and none has ever been falsified.

**Round 5 ran to completion and its findings are recorded.** It was dispatched against the pre-restatement claim text and restated claims landed while it read; it terminated after writing six findings (one major, `rr-r5-F1`), bound to `rev:98c94d02ef59f047`. Its verdict on the question this change's loop was about — whether the enumerated precedence was complete — was **yes**: it walked `suggestCandidateAction` and confirmed the enumeration covered every branch that can answer above the recurrence routes (the `reviewLoop` switch and all eight `invalidatingStates` entries). An earlier draft of this section said round 5 produced no report; that was written from an incomplete check of `tmp/` minutes before its report was written, and it is corrected here rather than left standing.

What the current revision *is* certified by (all of it under `.kata/**`, which no review request's reading set covers — a reviewer can read them from the task directory but cannot verify them from the request):
**What `approved | 0 findings` does not mean.** Rounds 3 and 4 raised three majors against earlier claim text. Their records bind revisions that have since been superseded, so they left the bar by *staleness*, not by disposition — the mechanism has no way to mark a superseded finding fixed (`findings dispose` does not exist), and the only route that replaces a finding's slot is a newer round's record. A reader who takes "approved with no findings" as "an independent round confirmed these repairs" would be reading something this change cannot claim.

The defect class behind the whole loop — a claim whose subject is the router's answer in a state, which every round could falsify by naming one more branch above it — is queued as **I-41** in `docs/engineering/2026-10-10-unified-kata-defect-queue.md`, with the mechanism-level remedy: a claim's subject may only be a branch, a derivation or a branch-local publication, never the router's output in a state.

## Boundaries

This change does not weaken strict review, close findings by assertion, fabricate a major finding, or alter the semantics of `stopLineCandidate`. It changes only the navigation consequence of a diagnostic when the repair ladder has no admissible repair.

It also does not touch the escalation above (`escalate_review_without_progress`), which already routes to `/kata-review`; the ordering that keeps the escalation and the invalidating states above the recurrence readings is unchanged.

The recurrence reading is still a *report about history* and still says nothing about whether the current revision is sound: where the ladder admits a repair the branches route on the historical reading even though the current round may hold a different problem. That is deliberate — the reason names what keeps coming back — and it is the same latitude the branches had before.
